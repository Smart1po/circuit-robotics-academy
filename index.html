What we teach

Four units run across every term. The same four for every age band — the depth changes, not the subject.

Unit 01

Build & Drive

Gear ratios, torque, and why a fast robot loses to a straight one. Students build a drivetrain, measure how far it actually travels per motor rotation, and rebuild it when the numbers disagree with the tape measure.

You leave with: a chassis that drives three metres straight, and a written record of how far off it was the first time.

Unit 02

Circuits & Soldering

Ohm's law on paper, then on a breadboard, then on a board they solder themselves. Multimeter first, iron second. Every student wires a working sensor board and can explain what each leg of it does before it gets power.

You leave with: a soldered sensor board you tested yourself.

Safety. Soldering happens at Bench B with fume extraction running and safety glasses on. SPARK students do the wiring; a coach holds the iron.

Unit 03

Sensors, Code & Vision

Reflectance sensors, ultrasonic distance and a gyro, and the code that makes them useful: line following with a proportional controller instead of a stack of if-statements, and a state machine for a mission run. APEX groups add a camera — colour thresholding first, then a small classifier running on the robot itself.

You leave with: a robot that follows a line without wobbling, and code you can explain line by line.

Unit 04

Drone Systems

Flight controller basics, prop pitch, centre of gravity, and a pre-flight check that gets done every single time. Flights happen in a netted indoor bay at low altitude.

You leave with: a completed pre-flight checklist and a supervised hover you flew yourself.

Safety. No student flies outdoors on our programme. Prop guards stay on. The bay net stays closed while a motor is armed.

How a term runs

Twelve weeks, in four stages. Nothing here is a one-off workshop — the robot a student starts in week one is the robot that runs in week twelve.

Stage 1 · Weeks 1–3 · Build

Unit 01. The chassis goes together, comes apart, and goes together better. By the end of week three every team has measured their own distance-per-rotation and written it in the logbook.

Stage 2 · Weeks 4–8 · Control

Units 02 and 03. Weeks four and five are the sensor board: wired, soldered, tested. Weeks six to eight are the control loop, where a robot stops doing what it was told and starts doing what it measures.

Stage 3 · Weeks 9–10 · Flight

Unit 04, in the Flight Bay. Same day, same time, different room. Two weeks on airframes, checks and a supervised hover.

Stage 4 · Weeks 11–12 · Run

Week eleven is a full dry run on the mat at competition timing, scored but not recorded. Week twelve is the real thing: two attempts, best one counts, families welcome from thirty minutes before.

Who it's for

Three bands, split by age rather than by experience. Nobody needs to arrive knowing how to code.

Competition track

A mat, six objectives, one hundred and fifty seconds, two attempts.

Our competition term uses an international-style autonomous mission format: a 2.4 by 1.2 metre mat, six scored objectives, a single 150-second round, and two attempts per team with the better attempt counting. Fully autonomous — once the round starts, nobody touches the robot.

Teams are two or three students. One person is nominated to place the robot and start the round; everyone else stays behind the line. The full scoring rubric, the penalties and the dry-run dates are in the members area.

Affiliation. We are not affiliated with any competition organiser, and we do not register students for external events. The format is ours, the mat is ours, and our coaches write and score it in-house. If a family wants to enter something outside CIRCUIT, we will help them prepare — that is as far as it goes.

Before you sign up

Do you enter students into competitions?

No. Our competition track is written and scored in-house. We are not affiliated with any competition organiser and we do not register students for external events.

What does a student take home?

Their logbook, their code and their build notes. Kits stay with CIRCUIT between terms; the deposit comes back when the kit does, complete.

Does my child need to know how to code?

No. SPARK starts in a block editor. RELAY moves to text. APEX writes the whole control loop. Nobody is expected to arrive with any of it.

Is there a trial session?

One per family, per band, in week one or two of a term. It is the same session everyone else is doing — we do not run a separate demo class, because a demo class does not tell you what a term is like.
